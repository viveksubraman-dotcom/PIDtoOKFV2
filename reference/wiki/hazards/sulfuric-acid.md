# Hazard: Sulfuric Acid — 98% (H₂SO₄)

> ⚠️ 98% sulfuric acid is a **skin and tissue contact corrosive** — causes severe, deep chemical burns on contact. Contact with water is **highly exothermic** — ALWAYS add acid to water, never add water to acid. Used in the CDN section as the acid catalyst for CHP cleavage (D-2310/D-2311/P-2305A–F system).

**Chemical formula**: H₂SO₄  
**Molecular weight**: 98.08 g/mol  
**CAS No.**: 7664-93-9  
**Concentration in service**: 98 wt% (concentrated)  
**Process section**: CDN — Acid injection for cleavage catalyst (D-2310/D-2311)  
**Source**: SDS_7664-93-9_sulfuric-acid-98pct.pdf

## Health Hazards

### Skin Contact — IMMEDIATE SEVERE BURNS
- Skin Corr 1A (H314): causes full-thickness chemical burns within seconds of contact
- 98% H₂SO₄ combines corrosive chemical action with **dehydration and heat of dilution** — burn severity exceeds weaker acid concentrations
- Flush immediately with large volumes of water; do NOT attempt to neutralise on skin (exothermic — worsens burns)

### Eye Contact
- Corrosive — causes permanent eye damage / blindness
- Flush with water minimum 20–30 minutes; hold eyelids open
- Immediate ophthalmological attention required

### Inhalation (Mist)
- H335: respiratory irritation at low levels; H314 for concentrated mist exposure
- Pulmonary oedema possible at high mist concentrations
- IDLH = 15 mg/m³ (acid mist) — evacuate if mist release detected
- OEL applies to **mist** — bulk liquid does not generate significant vapour at ambient; heating produces SO₃ mist

### Ingestion
- Corrosive — causes severe burns to mouth, throat, oesophagus, stomach
- Do NOT induce vomiting; do NOT give sodium bicarbonate (CO₂ release in stomach)
- Seek emergency medical care immediately

---

## Fire and Explosion Hazards

- **Non-flammable** — does not burn
- However, contact with **organic materials, combustibles, or reducing agents** generates heat and can ignite fires
- Contact with reactive metals (zinc, iron, aluminium) generates hydrogen gas — explosion hazard
- Mixing with water generates intense heat — steam explosion risk if water added to concentrated acid in bulk
- Fire involving H₂SO₄: use water flood (from safe distance) to dilute; evacuate area due to SO₂/SO₃ fumes

## Reactivity Hazards

| Incompatible With | Consequence |
|-----------------|------------|
| Water (rapid addition) | Violent exothermic reaction, steam/acid spray — ALWAYS add acid to water slowly |
| Strong bases (NaOH, Na₂CO₃) | Vigorous exothermic neutralisation — CO₂ release with carbonates |
| Organic materials (cumene, CHP, phenol, etc.) | Exothermic carbonisation; sulfonation reactions; risk of fire |
| CHP (concentrated) | Risk of catalytic CHP decomposition — acid concentration in cleavage circuit must be controlled tightly |
| Reactive metals (Zn, Al, Fe, Mg) | Hydrogen gas evolution — explosion risk |
| Chlorates, permanganates, perchlorates | Explosive oxidiser mixtures |
| Sodium carbonate / bicarbonate (neutralisation) | CO₂ evolution — do not perform in closed vessels |

> ⚠️ **Acid injection control (CDN section)**: Excess H₂SO₄ concentration in the decomposer (D-2304) leads to uncontrolled CHP decomposition. Acid injection rate and concentration in cleavage circuit are critical control parameters — see [[instruments/cause-effect-cdn]] and [[units/cleavage]].

## Emergency Response

### Spill
1. Evacuate area — use supplied-air if large spill with mist generation
2. Do NOT add water directly to large acid spill (exothermic / splashing)
3. For small spills: dilute slowly with large volumes of water from outside the spill area
4. Neutralise carefully with dry sodium carbonate or lime (apply slowly — CO₂ evolution)
5. Collect in labelled containers; dispose as hazardous waste

### Skin / Eye Contact
1. Flush with large volumes of water immediately — minimum 20 minutes (skin) / 20–30 minutes (eyes)
2. Do NOT neutralise on skin — water dilution only
3. Remove contaminated clothing during flushing
4. Seek emergency medical care immediately; inform physician of concentration (98%)

### Inhalation
1. Remove to fresh air; supplemental O₂ if breathing is difficult
2. Seek medical attention for any symptoms — delayed pulmonary oedema possible

## PPE Requirements

| Task | Minimum PPE |
|------|------------|
| Routine rounds near acid injection system | Safety glasses, face shield (in close approach); acid-resistant gloves |
| Sampling / connection operations | Full face shield, acid-resistant gloves and apron; air monitoring for mist |
| Maintenance of D-2310/D-2311/P-2305A–F | Full face shield, acid-resistant suit (PVC/neoprene), gloves, boot covers; SCBA if enclosed |
| Emergency response | Full SCBA, acid-resistant encapsulating suit (PVC or PTFE) |

**Glove material**: Neoprene, butyl rubber, or PVC for H₂SO₄ service; nitrile is NOT recommended for concentrated acid.

## Environmental Hazards

- pH depression of receiving water body — aquatic harm from acidic spills
- Neutralise all acid-contaminated wastewater before discharge
- Segregate from alkali waste streams — neutralisation in sewer prohibited (exothermic; CO₂ release)
