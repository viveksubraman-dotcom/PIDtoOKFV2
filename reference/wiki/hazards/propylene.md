# Hazard: Propylene / Propene (C₃H₆)

> ⚠️ Propylene is a **highly flammable gas** stored under pressure as liquefied gas. Rapid release or vessel failure can cause BLEVE (Boiling Liquid Expanding Vapour Explosion). Contact with liquid propylene causes **cryogenic frostbite** injury.

**Chemical formula**: CH₂=CH-CH₃ (C₃H₆)  
**Molecular weight**: 42.08 g/mol  
**CAS No.**: 115-07-1  
**Process section**: ALKY — Alkylation feedstock (propylene alkylates benzene to form cumene)  
**Source**: SDS_115-07-1_propylene.pdf

## Health Hazards

### Asphyxiation — PRIMARY CONCERN
- Simple asphyxiant: displaces oxygen in confined or poorly ventilated spaces
- No specific toxic effects — hazard is **oxygen depletion**
- Symptoms of O₂ deficiency: dizziness, headache, rapid breathing → unconsciousness → death
- Symptoms begin at ~16% O₂ (normal = 20.9%); immediately life-threatening at <10% O₂
- No odour warning at dangerous concentrations — mandatory O₂ monitoring in confined spaces and areas with propylene release potential

### Frostbite (Cryogenic Injury)
- Liquid propylene (at −47.7°C) causes **cryogenic frostbite** on contact with skin or eyes
- Do NOT allow liquid propylene to contact skin — insulating gloves ineffective unless rated for cryogenic service
- First aid: remove from cold source; do NOT rub frostbitten tissue; rewarm with warm water (max 40°C); seek immediate medical attention

### Narcosis (High Concentrations)
- At very high vapour concentrations (>10% v/v): simple narcosis possible — loss of consciousness
- This is secondary to the fire/explosion hazard at such concentrations

---

## Fire and Explosion Hazards

- **Flammable Gas 1 (H220)** — extremely flammable gas; ignites easily at ambient conditions
- LEL 2.1% / UEL 9.5% — wide flammable range
- Stored as liquefied gas under pressure — catastrophic vessel failure in fire → **BLEVE hazard**
  - BLEVE (Boiling Liquid Expanding Vapour Explosion): explosive vaporisation of propylene followed by fireball
  - Exclusion zone for BLEVE: per QRA/fire protection design — confirm blast radius with site SHE
- Gas release at ambient → immediate fire/explosion risk if ignition source present
- Vapour slightly heavier than air — may travel significant distances to ignition sources at grade level
- Firefighting: **Stop flow if possible** — do NOT extinguish propylene fire unless flow can be stopped (reignition will re-establish explosive cloud); cool surrounding vessels with water spray

## Emergency Response

### Gas Release / Leak
1. Evacuate area; eliminate all ignition sources
2. Do NOT attempt to stop leak unless safe to do so — withdraw personnel
3. Ventilate area; monitor for O₂ depletion and LEL
4. Apply water spray to cool any propylene storage vessel or pipework exposed to heat / fire
5. Alert fire brigade and isolate supply

### Fire Involving Propylene
1. **Do NOT extinguish burning propylene** unless the source flow can be stopped — unburned propylene cloud will reform and reignite explosively
2. Cool surrounding structures and vessels with water spray from maximum safe distance
3. Establish large exclusion zone — BLEVE potential if vessel is fire-exposed
4. Allow to burn under controlled conditions while stopping supply; call fire brigade

### Person Contaminated
1. Asphyxiation: remove to fresh air; apply O₂ if available; CPR if not breathing
2. Frostbite: remove from cold contact; do NOT rub; warm with tepid water (35–40°C); seek emergency medical care
