## Chapter 6-1: Effectiveness of PHA/HAZOP Meeting

### Hidden Costs of a Poorly Run PHA
- Study runs shorter or longer than necessary
- Unnecessary recommendations are generated
- Significant hazards may be missed or dismissed
- Team demoralization and lack of "buy-in" may occur
- Unhappy management
- Missed operability improvements

### Components of an Effective Meeting
- Agreement on scope
- Agreement on method
- Roles understood by all
- Someone in charge: directing workflow, creating a productive environment, controlling the output
  - *Ideally the person in charge has no direct stake in the conclusions/outcome*
- Expertise was fully utilized
- Promote open discussion
- Team commits to the PHA/HAZOP output/conclusions

## Chapter 6-2: Decision Making

### Ways a HAZOP Leader Can Get Decisions Made
(Listed from least to most delegation/facilitation)

| Level | Approach |
|-------|---------|
| 1 | **Decide and tell team** — Leader decides unilaterally |
| 2 | **Get input from a few, then decide** — Selective consultation |
| 3 | **Consensus decision** — Full team agreement *(preferred)* |
| 4 | **Decision delegated to team** — Leader establishes the process, team decides |

### Importance of Consensus
- Usually results in better decisions
- Team more committed to conclusions — better able to "sell" them later
- No "corridor politicking" by the disgruntled
- Fewer confrontations in meetings
- Win-Win situation

> "The above can be difficult to achieve when dealing with an EPCC and an Owner"

## Chapter 6-3: Role of Facilitator

### Core Facilitator Behaviors
- Establishes ground rules
- Ensures adherence to roles
- Fanatically attentive to meeting process, content, and schedule
- Creates and rigorously maintains a "safe" workshop environment
- Remains as neutral as possible; contributes ideas as a member, not as a leader
- Maintains high energy level
- Keeps the process moving forward (lobbies for more time if needed for quality output)

### Progressive Role of the HAZOP Leader

The HAZOP Leader must fulfill four progressive roles during the study:

```
Trainer  →  Team Builder  →  Facilitator  →  Editor
```

#### Role 1: Trainer
*At the start of the study / first session*
- Train the team in the HAZOP technique
- Let the group create the ground rules themselves
- Explain the leader's function
- Contribute ideas initially — help guide the team into HAZOP patterns
- Phrase technical contributions as questions (not statements of fact)

#### Role 2: Team Builder
*Early sessions — establishing participation*
- Get everyone used to contributing by going around the room on "causes"
- Call on people with specific expertise to provide input to the issue at hand
- Encourage, on occasion
- Direct questions: e.g., "John, do you see any other consequences here?"
- Ask people to explain further if a point is not clear
- Repel all attacks (gently at first) and maintain a "safe environment"

#### Role 3: Facilitator
*During node analysis sessions*
- Does not evaluate others' ideas — refers them to the team
- Does not abuse facilitator's power
- Uses flip charts / electronic "parking lot" to capture issues not documented by the Scribe
- Remains as neutral as possible (unless required to "step in")
- Deals with difficult personalities

#### Role 4: Editor
*(Implied from diagram — detailed content not provided in this chapter)*
- Review and quality-check the HAZOP worksheet and recommendations during/after sessions

### Typical HAZOP Ground Rules
*Leader lets the group help create these, but these are the standard starting set:*

**Core ground rules (9):**
1. Start on time and end on time
2. Take breaks regularly to maintain high team energy
3. All ideas are worthy — no bad ideas
4. Safe environment (no personal attacks)
5. Everyone contributes
6. **Do not design solutions in the HAZOP meeting** — identify the problem, raise a recommendation; do not solve it here
7. Take conflicts outside the workshop
8. Phones on silent / vibrate / off
9. Advise of any absence in advance

**Supplementary ground rules (4):**
1. Diversity is good
2. Present your view but avoid arguing for it
3. Listen to others and look for compromise
4. Do not change your view simply to avoid conflict

### Maintaining Energy Level
- Get up and walk around the room
- Keep up the pace
- Call on people by name with open-ended questions (e.g., "Mary, what are some more safeguards for this?")
- If group gets stuck: circle the room until everyone gives attention, then recap
- Listen; do not cut anyone off
- Point out progress — reward group for milestones, but do not lie
- Use humor appropriately

### More Facilitator Tips
- Encourage ideas with open palms (positive body language)
- Regain focus by moving to the middle of the room or next to the Scribe, then summarize
- Repeatedly make eye contact with everyone
- Do not answer questions directly — bounce them back to the group
- Move flow along by stepping in at the right moment: e.g., "Thanks Jill, Jack, what thoughts do you have on the consequences here?"
- Avoid negative body language:
  - Do not roll your eyes or shrug when someone proposes an idea
  - Do not turn your back on someone unintentionally

## Chapter 6-4: Active Listening

Three active listening techniques for the HAZOP Leader:

| Technique | Purpose | How |
|-----------|---------|-----|
| **Complete Attention** | Encourage contribution | Give full undivided attention; acknowledge and thank for input; this encourages more sharing |
| **Paraphrasing** | Show understanding; check comprehension | Repeat what was said in your own words; confirm your understanding of what was said |
| **Summarizing** | Confirm key substance | Like paraphrasing, but focuses on establishing and confirming the key elements / main message |
