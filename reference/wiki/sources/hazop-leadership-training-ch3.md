## What is HAZOP?

- **Systematic technique** to identify potential hazards and operating problems
- **Qualitative** technique based on "guide-words" that provoke thoughts about deviations from intended operating conditions
- Deviations are developed by applying guide-words to process parameters
- **Primarily for safety:** hazards are the main concern; operability problems are secondary (affect product quality, production rate, profit)

## Purpose and Objectives

HAZOP emphasises operating integrity across all operating modes:
- Normal steady-state operations
- Start-up and shut-down

**HAZOP objectives:**
1. Identify hazards and operability problems
2. Assess the extent of the hazard or operability problem
3. Identify existing safeguards for each hazardous event
4. Propose recommendations to prevent or mitigate consequences
5. Serve as input to risk management activities

---

## How and Why HAZOP is Used

- **Team effort:** plant/process designers, operating personnel, control and instrument engineers, safety engineers
- Identifies causes and consequences of mal-operations of equipment and operator interfaces within the complete system
- Encourages creativity in design concept evaluation
- Produces fewer commissioning and operational problems
- Provides a management tool: demonstrates to insurers and inspectors evidence of comprehensive thoroughness
- HAZOP reports are integral to plant safety records and applicable to design changes/modifications throughout the plant's operating lifetime

## HAZOP Limitations

| Limitation | Implication for This Study |
|-----------|--------------------------|
| Not suitable for layout/quantification issues | Quantitative risk assessment (QRA) is a separate activity |
| Not a means to define engineering solutions | HAZOP identifies; engineering resolves (separate action items) |
| Not for solving problems during the session | Recommendations are generated; resolution is post-study |
| Not for challenging design conditions per se | Though design challenges that arise must still be faced |
| Time-consuming; no shortcuts permitted | Full-record approach required per [[hazop/study-info]] |
| System must be well-defined | PSI completeness required — see [[sources/Table-A6.2-2-PSI-readiness-checklist]] |
| Quality depends on PSI accuracy | PSI gaps identified in wiki must be resolved before node analysis |
| Quality depends on team expertise | HAZOP Leader qualification requirements per [[sources/P-Q-MP-OEMS-005]] §3.1.4 |

## Basic Features of a HAZOP

- Structured/systematic examination of a process or procedure (planned or existing)
- Guideword-stimulated brainstorming
- Team of subject matter experts (5–7 members)
- Led by an experienced, qualified HAZOP Leader
- Identifies potential safety and operability problems
- Recommends actions to resolve — not a design meeting
- Asks: "Is it safe?" / "Can it be operated as intended?" / If NOT: "How can the design be improved?"
