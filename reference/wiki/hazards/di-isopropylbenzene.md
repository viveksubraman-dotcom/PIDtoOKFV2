# Hazard: Di-isopropylbenzene / DIPB (C₁₂H₁₈)

> ⚠️ DIPB (para-diisopropylbenzene, CAS 100-18-5) is a by-product of the alkylation section formed by di-alkylation of benzene. Data quality flags exist for aspiration hazard (H304) and aquatic toxicity (H411) — verify against ECHA C&L before finalising PSI. See data quality notes below.

**Chemical formula**: C₆H₄[CH(CH₃)₂]₂ (C₁₂H₁₈)  
**Common name**: Para-diisopropylbenzene (p-DIPB)  
**Molecular weight**: 162.27 g/mol  
**CAS No.**: 100-18-5 (para isomer)  
**Process section**: ALKY (poly-alkylation by-product; recycled or purged)  
**Source**: SDS_100-18-5_di-isopropylbenzene.pdf

## Health Hazards

### Aspiration (H304 — precautionary PSS basis, confirmed 2026-06-14)
- **Do NOT induce vomiting** after ingestion — aspiration pneumonia risk (H304 applied as PSS conservative basis)
- Seek medical attention immediately if ingested
- Low vapour pressure means inhalation risk at ambient is negligible

### Skin / Eye
- Mild skin and eye irritant on prolonged contact
- Not significantly absorbed through skin

### Chronic
- Limited data; apply ALARA principle for chronic cumulative aromatic hydrocarbon exposure

---

## Fire and Explosion Hazards

- Flash point ≥82°C — combustible liquid (Class IIIB); low fire risk at ambient temperatures
- Low vapour pressure at ambient — vapour ignition hazard is limited below flash point
- High boiling point limits the vapour generation rate even when heated
- Firefighting: CO₂, dry chemical, foam, water spray for cooling

## Emergency Response

### Spill
1. Contain with inert absorbent; collect in labelled containers
2. Prevent entry into drains / watercourses (aquatic hazard concern — verify H411)

### Fire
1. CO₂, dry chemical, foam, or water spray
2. Low fire hazard at ambient temperature

### Person Contaminated
1. Skin: wash with soap and water; remove contaminated clothing
2. Eyes: flush with water minimum 15 minutes; seek medical attention if irritation persists
3. **Ingestion: DO NOT induce vomiting** if H304 (aspiration hazard) is confirmed — seek medical attention immediately
4. Inhalation: move to fresh air; symptoms resolve in fresh air

## Environmental Hazards

- **H411 (Aquatic Chronic 2) applied as precautionary PSS basis** (confirmed 2026-06-14) — contain all spills; do NOT allow DIPB to enter drains, watercourses, or soil
- Route to [[units/etp]] for all liquid releases
- Aromatic hydrocarbon — monitor soil/groundwater if significant land release occurs
