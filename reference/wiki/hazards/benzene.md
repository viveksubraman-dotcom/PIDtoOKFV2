# Hazard: Benzene (C₆H₆)

> ⚠️ Benzene is a **confirmed human carcinogen** (IARC Group 1). There is no safe exposure level — minimise all contact and maintain exposures as low as reasonably achievable (ALARA). Biological monitoring (urinary S-PMA or t,t-MA) is required for workers with routine exposure.

**Chemical formula**: C₆H₆  
**Molecular weight**: 78.11 g/mol  
**CAS No.**: 71-43-2  
**Process section**: ALKY (Alkylation) — primary feedstock  
**Source**: SDS_71-43-2_benzene.pdf

## Health Hazards

### Carcinogenicity — PRIMARY LONG-TERM CONCERN
- IARC Group 1 (H350 Carc 1A): confirmed human carcinogen — acute myeloid leukaemia (AML), non-Hodgkin lymphoma
- No safe threshold — any level of exposure carries risk; minimise ALARA
- **Biological monitoring required**: urinary S-phenylmercapturic acid (S-PMA, ≤25 µg/g creatinine per ACGIH BEI) and/or urinary t,t-muconic acid — confirm Thailand regulatory requirement

### Haematotoxicity (Acute and Chronic)
- STOT RE 1 (H372): target organ — blood-forming organs (bone marrow)
- Chronic exposure: aplastic anaemia, bone marrow suppression, pancytopenia
- Acute high exposure: CNS narcosis, headache, dizziness, nausea, loss of consciousness

### Acute Exposure
- Inhalation: dizziness, headache, nausea, CNS depression; high concentrations — unconsciousness, death
- Skin: irritant (H315); prolonged contact causes dermatitis; low absorption rate but cumulative dose matters
- Eye: irritant (H319); flush immediately
- Ingestion: toxic — do not induce vomiting (aspiration hazard)

## Fire and Explosion Hazards

- **Flash point −11°C** — flammable at ambient temperature; treat as a permanent fire hazard in storage and transfer operations
- Vapour density 2.7 — heavier than air; vapour travels to ignition sources along floor/trenches
- LEL 1.2% / UEL 7.8% — wide flammable range
- Firefighting: CO₂, dry chemical, foam (alcohol-resistant); water spray to cool containers but not effective to extinguish
- Prevent vapour accumulation in enclosed spaces; use explosion-proof electrical equipment

## Emergency Response

### Spill
1. Evacuate area; eliminate all ignition sources (including static)
2. Ventilate area — vapour is heavier than air and collects in sumps/drains
3. Contain with inert absorbent (dry sand, vermiculite); collect in closed containers for disposal
4. Use explosion-proof equipment; ground and bond containers during transfer

### Fire
1. Evacuate non-essential personnel
2. CO₂, dry chemical, or foam for small fires; water spray for cooling containers
3. Withdraw personnel if containers are exposed to fire — BLEVE risk from pressurised vessels

### Person Contaminated
1. Inhalation: move to fresh air; supplemental O₂ if breathing is difficult
2. Skin: flush with soap and water for minimum 15 minutes; remove contaminated clothing
3. Eyes: flush with water for minimum 15 minutes; seek medical attention
4. **Report all benzene exposures** — medical surveillance and biological monitoring required

## PPE Requirements

| Task | Minimum PPE |
|------|------------|
| Routine monitoring / rounds | Safety glasses, standard PPE; gas monitor (PID or FID) |
| Sampling / connection operations | Chemical-resistant gloves, face shield, organic vapour respirator (if OEL exceedance risk) |
| Maintenance / draining | Full face shield, chemical-resistant gloves and suit, SCBA or supplied-air if confined |
| Emergency response | Full SCBA, chemical-resistant encapsulating suit |

**Medical surveillance**: Annual blood count (CBC) + liver function tests required for workers with routine benzene exposure. Working OEL = **0.02 ppm** (PSS conservative basis). Biological monitoring: urinary S-PMA (≤25 µg/g creatinine, ACGIH BEI) and/or t,t-muconic acid. Confirm Thailand Department of Labour Protection & Welfare regulatory requirements for benzene medical surveillance.

## Environmental Hazards

- Aquatic Chronic 2 (H411) — toxic to aquatic life with long-lasting effects
- Moderately biodegradable under aerobic conditions
- Monitor soil and groundwater for benzene contamination at transfer and storage areas
- All benzene-contaminated runoff to [[units/etp]] before discharge
