# Hazard: Phenol (C₆H₅OH)

> ⚠️ Phenol is absorbed rapidly through intact skin — even small exposures without pain can cause systemic toxicity (cardiac arrhythmia, seizures, coma, death). This is a **silent killer** hazard.

**Chemical formula**: C₆H₅OH  
**Molecular weight**: 94.11 g/mol  
**CAS No.**: 108-95-2  
**EINECS**: 203-632-7  
**Source**: SDS_108-95-2_phenol.pdf

## Health Hazards

### Skin Absorption — PRIMARY CONCERN

Phenol is absorbed through intact skin **rapidly and silently**:
- Contact causes only mild local numbness (anaesthetic effect) — **no pain warning**
- Systemic absorption: cardiac arrhythmia, CNS depression, seizures, coma, death
- A splash to >25% body surface area can be lethal within minutes
- Concentrated phenol causes white/brown chemical burns after contact
- **Skin notation** (ACGIH): dermal contribution to total body burden is significant — wear full PPE even if vapour levels are below TLV

### Inhalation
- Vapours irritate upper respiratory tract; strong phenolic odour provides early warning
- High concentrations: pulmonary oedema, CNS depression
- Immediately Dangerous to Life and Health (IDLH) = 250 ppm

### Eye Contact
- Corrosive (H314) — causes severe burns and permanent eye damage
- Flush immediately for minimum 20 minutes; hold eyelids open
- Do NOT wear contact lenses in phenol areas

### Ingestion
- Highly toxic (H301) — fatal dose for adults ~1 g/kg body weight
- Do not induce vomiting; seek medical attention immediately

### Chronic / Repeat Exposure
- STOT RE 2 (H373) — possible kidney damage from repeated exposure
- Mutagenicity 2 (H341) — suspect mutagen; IARC Group 3 (not classifiable for carcinogenicity)

## Fire and Explosion Hazards

- Combustible liquid (flash point 79°C — Class IIIB)
- Can form explosive vapour/air mixtures above flash point
- Phenol solidifies at ~41°C — heat tracing on all product lines must be maintained to prevent blockages
- Combustion products: CO, CO₂ (no unusual decomposition products at normal fire temperatures)
- Firefighting agents: water spray / fog, CO₂, dry chemical, alcohol-resistant foam

## Emergency Response

### Skin Contact — URGENT
1. Remove ALL contaminated clothing immediately (including shoes and socks)
2. Flush with large volumes of water for **minimum 20 minutes**
3. Do NOT use alcohol (increases dermal absorption)
4. Apply polyethylene glycol (PEG 300 or PEG 400) if available — absorbs phenol from skin surface
5. Call emergency medical services **immediately**
6. **Transport to hospital even if no symptoms** — systemic absorption delay possible; cardiac monitoring required

### Eye Contact
1. Flush with water for minimum 20 minutes; hold eyelids open
2. Do NOT wear contact lenses in phenol areas
3. Seek ophthalmological attention immediately

### Inhalation
1. Remove to fresh air
2. If breathing is difficult, apply supplemental oxygen
3. Medical attention required if symptoms persist

### Spill
1. Phenol solidifies below ~41°C — heated equipment/warm water required for cleanup
2. Contain with sand or dry earth
3. Collect in sealed drums for disposal
4. Phenolic wastewater must not enter general drains — route to [[units/etp]]
5. UN 1671 (solid) or UN 2312 (molten) — use appropriate hazmat labelling for transport

## PPE Requirements

| Task | Minimum PPE |
|------|------------|
| Routine rounds (area) | Safety glasses, standard PPE; gas monitor in enclosed areas |
| Sampling | Face shield, neoprene or butyl rubber gloves, chemical-resistant apron |
| Maintenance / draining | Full face shield, chemical-resistant suit (butyl or neoprene), gloves, boot covers |
| Emergency response | Full SCBA, encapsulating chemical suit |

**Glove material**: Nitrile gloves provide only SHORT breakthrough protection against phenol (~15–30 min). Use butyl rubber or neoprene for extended contact.

## Environmental Hazards

- Aquatic Chronic 3 (H412) — harmful to aquatic life with long-lasting effects
- Phenol in wastewater creates high COD/BOD load
- Route all phenolic wastewater to [[units/etp]] before discharge
- Thai industrial effluent standards (DIW) apply — check site permit limits

## Plant-Specific Notes

- Heat tracing: all phenol-service lines and equipment require steam or electric heat tracing
- Annual heat tracing survey recommended
