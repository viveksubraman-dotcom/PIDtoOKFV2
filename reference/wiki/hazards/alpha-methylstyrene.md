# Hazard: Alpha-Methylstyrene / AMS (C₉H₁₀)

> ⚠️ AMS can **polymerise exothermically** if inhibitor (TBC/MEHQ) is depleted or if exposed to heat, peroxides, or strong oxidisers. Check inhibitor levels in AMS storage and recycle streams. AMS is a by-product of CHP cleavage and must be managed in the DIST section.

**Chemical formula**: C₆H₅C(CH₃)=CH₂ (C₉H₁₀)  
**IUPAC name**: α-Methylstyrene; 2-Phenyl-1-propene  
**Molecular weight**: 118.18 g/mol  
**CAS No.**: 98-83-9  
**Process section**: CDN (cleavage by-product), DIST (AMS column)  
**Source**: SDS_98-83-9_alpha-methylstyrene.pdf

## Health Hazards

### Acute Effects
- H302 (Acute Tox 4): harmful if swallowed
- Vapour inhalation: CNS narcosis, dizziness, headache at high concentrations
- Eye and skin: irritant; prolonged contact causes mild dermatitis
- Possible aspiration hazard (H304, if confirmed — see data quality flag above)

### Reproductive Toxicity (H361 — precautionary PSS basis)
- H361 (Repr 2): applied as **precautionary working basis for PSS** per confirmation 2026-06-14
- Women of childbearing age must minimise AMS exposure — consider reassignment for pregnant workers
- **Report any reproductive health concerns** to occupational health immediately
- Confirm final H361 status against ECHA C&L before formal PSI sign-off

### Aspiration Hazard (H304 — precautionary PSS basis)
- H304 (Asp Tox 1): applied as **precautionary working basis for PSS** per confirmation 2026-06-14
- **Do NOT induce vomiting** after ingestion — aspiration pneumonia risk
- Seek medical attention immediately if ingested

### Skin Sensitisation (Skin Sens 1B — precautionary PSS basis)
- Applied as precautionary PSS basis — any skin reaction after AMS contact to be reported immediately
- First exposure sensitisation may not cause reaction; subsequent exposures may cause allergic response
- Medical surveillance for workers with routine AMS exposure recommended

## Fire and Explosion Hazards

- Flash point 53°C — combustible liquid; hazard increases above 53°C
- Wide vapour at elevated process temperatures — LEL 1.1%
- Inhibitor-depleted AMS may polymerise and generate heat — **monitor inhibitor levels**
- Firefighting: CO₂, dry chemical, foam (alcohol-resistant if AMS in water mixture); water spray for cooling

---

## Reactivity Hazards

| Incompatible With | Consequence |
|-----------------|------------|
| Heat / peroxides / strong oxidisers | Initiates **exothermic polymerisation** |
| Strong acids | May catalyse polymerisation; potential sulfonation |
| Inhibitor depletion | Spontaneous polymerisation risk in storage/recycle |
| Air (oxygen depletion of inhibitor) | Inhibitor (TBC) requires O₂ to function — do NOT store under inert atmosphere without alternative stabiliser |

> ⚠️ **TBC inhibitor requires dissolved oxygen to function.** If AMS is stored under nitrogen blanketing (reduced O₂), TBC becomes ineffective — use an alternative inhibitor (MEHQ) for inerted storage. Confirm inhibitor system with operations.

## Emergency Response

### Spill
1. Evacuate area; eliminate ignition sources
2. Contain with inert absorbent; avoid contact with skin
3. Prevent entry into drains / watercourses
4. Collect for disposal as flammable/reactive waste

### Fire
1. CO₂, dry chemical, or foam; water spray for cooling
2. Inform fire brigade of polymerisation risk if containers are heated

### Person Contaminated
1. Inhalation: move to fresh air; supplemental O₂ if needed
2. Skin: wash with soap and water; remove contaminated clothing
3. Eyes: flush with water minimum 15 minutes
4. Ingestion: do not induce vomiting; seek medical attention
